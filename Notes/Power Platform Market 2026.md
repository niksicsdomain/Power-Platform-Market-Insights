# The Power Platform Gap Nobody Talks About

There's a line that's been circulating in the Microsoft community lately that deserves more attention:
"Most developers don't understand low-code. Most low-code builders don't understand enterprise."
It sounds simple. But it describes one of the most significant gaps in the Power Platform market right now — and it creates a genuine opportunity for the people who sit in the middle of it.

What the gap actually means:

On one side you have traditional developers — strong in code, weak in low-code thinking. They underestimate the platform, overlook what it can do natively, and reach for custom code 
when the platform already solves the problem.
On the other side you have citizen developers — capable of building flows and apps, but without the enterprise foundation. No understanding of governance, ALM, security architecture, 
or how to design something that scales beyond a single team.
The middle ground — someone who understands the Power Platform deeply AND has delivered in regulated, enterprise environments — is genuinely rare. That means Dataverse data modelling, 
security roles and business units, ALM pipelines across Dev, UAT, and Production, Azure integration patterns, and governance frameworks that actually hold up under scrutiny.
That's not a citizen developer who built a few flows. That's a different profile entirely.

Why this matters in 2026:

The freelance market data backs it up. Over 2,000 Power Automate jobs posted on Upwork in the last 30 days. 500+ Power Apps gigs active on Fiverr. 1,000+ Power Platform postings on 
LinkedIn. Demand growing at 40% year over year.
The market isn't short of people who can build. It's short of people who can build to an enterprise standard — with the governance, security, and architectural thinking that regulated 
clients actually need.

The certification signal:

Certifications like PL-200 matter in this context not because they teach you what you already know — but because they signal to the market that you know it. In a crowded freelance 
landscape where clients can't easily assess depth, a Microsoft certification is the shorthand for credibility.

The takeaway:

If you've been delivering Power Platform solutions in enterprise or government environments and you're not yet thinking about the freelance market — the timing is worth reconsidering.
The gap is real, the demand is measurable, and the people who position themselves in the middle of it now are building something that compounds over time.
